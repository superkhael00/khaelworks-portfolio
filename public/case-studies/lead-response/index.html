The problem, and what this solves

Problem

Small businesses lose deals not because the offer is weak, but because follow-up is slow. A lead fills out a form, and nothing happens for hours or days — by the time someone replies, the prospect has already talked to a competitor.

Solution

The moment a lead arrives, it's checked for duplicates, acknowledged by email, logged to Trello with a priority label and a 6-item follow-up checklist, and — if it's high priority — flagged to the sales team on Slack instantly.

Design principles

A

Automate the repetitive, not the judgment

Data entry, notifications, and status tracking are automated. Deciding who owns a lead, and doing the actual selling, stays human.

B

Fail safely

Duplicate detection requires a meaningful combination of matching fields, not one, so a legitimate new lead is never blocked by accident.

C

Build for real usage

The lifecycle sync watches the whole board with one trigger, instead of needing a separate automation per pipeline stage.

Case study — BrightPath Sales Operations (simulated client)

A small web, SEO, and paid-ads shop previously ran inbound leads through a form, a manual inbox check, and a spreadsheet someone remembered to update.

Before
  • Leads sat unacknowledged for hours or days
  • No consistent way to flag urgent inquiries
  • Follow-up tasks lived in memory, not a system
  • The spreadsheet was often out of date
After
  • Every lead is acknowledged within seconds
  • High-priority leads alert the team on Slack instantly
  • Every lead gets a 6-step follow-up checklist automatically
  • The sheet updates itself as deals move — always accurate

Frequently asked

Does this replace a CRM?

Not fully — it turns Trello and Google Sheets into a lightweight, CRM-style system. For a business not ready to pay for or learn a full CRM, this covers most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost.

What if two leads submit the same info?

The system checks for a matching combination of contact details before creating a new entry, and flags likely duplicates instead of doubling up the Trello card and the alert.

Can this connect to tools other than Trello, Sheets, Gmail, and Slack?

Yes. Make.com connects to well over a thousand apps, so this same capture → route → notify → sync pattern can be rebuilt around whatever a business already uses — HubSpot, Notion, Monday.com, Microsoft Teams.

How long does setup take for a new client?

Once the core logic is proven, as it is here, adapting it to a new business's tools and pipeline stages typically takes a few days — most of the time goes into understanding their workflow, not rebuilding the automation.

What happens if Trello or Sheets goes down?

Make.com queues or retries the failed step depending on the error type. No data is silently lost, though a very prolonged outage would need a manual review.
